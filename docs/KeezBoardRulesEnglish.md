# How to play
You play Keezen in pairs. The duos are determined among the players. Each player chooses their own color to play with. Your partner sits diagonally across from you and the player in the middle sits opposite you (with six players). The cards determine how many steps you may move with your pawn. The game is played in rounds. In the first round, each player receives five cards; in the second and third rounds, each player receives four cards. After three rounds, all cards are collected again and you play three new rounds.

# Goal
The goal of the game is to get all of your pawns into your home as quickly as possible together with your partner. You win Keezen when both of you are in your own home. To win, you must help each other! If one of the two players has all four of his pawns in his home, he continues with the pawns of his partner.

# The game
To begin the game, you must first have an ace or king in your hand (= set up); otherwise, you must discard your cards.

A designated player shuffles the cards and the player to their left throws a card of their choice onto the table and moves a pawn. In turn, each player throws a card onto the table, continuing until all cards are used. Then the cards are dealt again by the player who was allowed to throw a card first.

The next player continues the game. You must throw a card, even if it means you have to discard yourself or your partner. If you truly cannot, because for example you only have kings left and nothing else to set up with, you must discard all remaining cards and wait for the next round. When your pawn is in its starting position, no one may pass it and no one may capture your pawn. This pawn may also not be swapped if someone has a jack. If you yourself have a jack, you may swap, except with someone who is also in their starting position. For the other cards, you move as many steps as shown on the card. You must connect your pawns in your home. But you may not jump over a pawn that is already in your home. To get your last pawn inside, you may also use a seven. If your partner is not yet inside, the remainder of the seven must go to your partner.

# Example
Jan needs a three to get his last pawn inside. He does not have one, but he does have a seven. Jan brings his last pawn inside by moving three spaces and then continuing with one of his partner's pawns for the remaining four spaces. From now on they play together with the same pawns, which are the partner's pawns of Jan.

# Cards and their meaning
- Ace: Place a new pawn on the board or move one space forward.
- King: Place a new pawn on the board.
- Queen: Move twelve spaces forward.
- Jack: Swap one of your own pawns with your teammate or opponent. (Warning! You may not swap a pawn if it is on a square of its own color)
- Seven: You may split the seven across 2 pawns. For example, three spaces with one pawn and four spaces with the other.
- Four: Move four spaces backward. (Warning! You may not move backward into your home.)
- Other cards: For the rest, the number of steps is the value shown on the card (2 = 2, 3 = 3, etc.)

# The board
see [keezboard-2-4-6-1-600x394.jpg](keezboard-2-4-6-1-600x394.jpg) Per player there is a section with 16 board positions. One of them is the starting position. This position is colored. Here the player places the pawn after playing an ace or a king. There are also 4 home positions. They are connected to the board position just before the starting position. These are the positions where the player must get its pawns in. Finally there are 4 waiting positions. Here the pawns are placed before they are player.
